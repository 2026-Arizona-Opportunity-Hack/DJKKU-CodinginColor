Part 1 · The story

1. In one page

Coding in Color Learn is a free, self-paced learning platform for Coding in Color's high school coding classes. It lets CIC write a lesson once, teach it in any partner school, and prove with numbers how much students learned.

What it does

  • Students work through modules at their own pace: video, reading, practice questions, real Python in the browser, and a pre-test and post-test. Each step unlocks after the one before it.
  • Instructors see who is stuck, who has not started and what to reteach, without a spreadsheet, and print a one-page impact report.
  • CIC staff write and publish lessons in the browser, find videos and readings in seconds, and let AI draft the tests.
  • CIC's own dashboard pulls completion and learning gain per school through a keyed API.

Where it stands

  • Live at cic-demo-6br.pages.dev, on the free tiers of Supabase and Cloudflare: $0 a month, no credit card.
  • About 800 automated tests run on every change, including 71 that check the database's access rules role by role on a real database.
  • Accessibility is checked automatically: the build fails if Lighthouse scores a page below 95.
  • Students' privacy is built in: a first name and last initial only, no email, no password.
The measure that matters: learning gain. Every module has a pre-test and a post-test with matched questions. Learning gain is the post-test score minus the pre-test score, per student, per concept and per class. In the demo data, one class went from 29% to 74% on the same questions: a gain of 46 points. This is the number CIC needs for grant reports, and the platform calculates it on its own.

2. The problem we were asked to solve

Coding in Color teaches coding to high school students, many from groups under-represented in tech, through classes at partner schools, camps and an AI Academy. Before this platform, lessons lived in slides and documents, progress lived in spreadsheets, and showing funders what students learned meant counting by hand.

CIC asked for one place to do four things:

  1. Write curriculum once and reuse it in every class, with versions, so a lesson can improve without disrupting a class in the middle of it.
  2. Let students learn at their own pace, on school Chromebooks or a phone at home, without accounts or emails for minors.
  3. Show instructors who needs help while there is still time to help them.
  4. Measure learning, not just attendance, and report it per school.

All four are built and working. Section 12 lists every feature and its status.

3. How a lesson becomes a result

The whole system follows one path. Every chapter in Part 2 is one stop on it.

1. WriteAn author builds a module: lessons, videos, readings, practice and Python. AI drafts the pre-test and post-test.
→
2. PublishA CIC admin reviews it and publishes it. Published versions are locked.
→
3. Set upAn instructor creates a class, adds students and assigns the module.
→
4. LearnStudents sign in with a code, take the pre-test, work through the lessons and take the post-test.
→
5. ActThe instructor sees who is stuck and what to reteach, and helps.
→
6. ReportLearning gain per student, class and school, printed or pulled by CIC's dashboard.

Two rules hold the path together. First, one module, many classes: CIC writes a module once and any class at any partner school can use it, while each class keeps its own progress and results. Second, versions never change under a class: when CIC improves a module, it publishes a new version, and classes already using the old one keep it until an instructor switches them.

Part 2 · Using it, step by step

5. Getting started

Open cic-demo-6br.pages.dev in any modern browser: a school Chromebook, a laptop or a phone. Nothing needs installing. The Guide link in the menu lists every feature for your role and takes you straight to it; it is the fastest way to find anything in this chapter.

You areTo get in
A student with a codeGo to Sign in, type the code from your instructor in the student box. No email, no password.
A student with a class linkOpen the link or scan the QR code your instructor shows, then choose your name.
A visitorChoose Try a free lesson on the home page. No account needed.
Staff (instructor, author, admin, reviewer)Go to Sign in, choose Sign in with Google and use the Google account CIC invited.
The first CIC admin is set up once, by whoever deploys the system (Part 5, section 18). After that, every other staff member is invited from the admin console.

6. CIC admins: run the program

As a CIC admin you see everything and can use every other role's screens too. The Admin menu has four tabs.

Invite staff

  1. Open Admin → Overview and find Invite staff.
  2. Type their Google email, choose a role (Instructor, External reviewer or CIC admin) and, for an instructor who will write modules, tick Allow curriculum authoring.
  3. Press Invite staff. They get the role the first time they sign in with Google using that email. If they had signed in before, it applies at once.

Staff a class and see every program

Overview shows the number of partner schools, classes and staff, and a table of every class with its school, term and instructors. A class with nobody teaching it is marked No instructor. Use Class staffing to add an instructor to any class.

Change a role or remove access

  1. Open Admin → Staff. Each person has a role menu and, for instructors, an authoring switch.
  2. To take someone's access away, press Remove access. The confirmation lists any class that would be left without an instructor.
  3. Press Yes, remove access. They lose every staff screen at once. Their account, the modules they wrote and their history stay, and inviting them again gives access back.

Under Invitations, Cancel invite withdraws an invite nobody has used, for example one sent to the wrong address.

Publish curriculum

  1. Open Admin → Publish queue. Modules an author submitted are under Waiting for review.
  2. Read the module. Press Publish to make it available to every class, or Send back to return it to the author as a draft.
  3. If something is missing (for example a lesson with no activities), the system says exactly what to fix and does not publish.

Give CIC's dashboard a key

  1. Open Admin → API keys, name the key (for example "CIC dashboard") and choose what it may read: completion, learning gain or both.
  2. Press Make key and copy it. It is shown only once; store it in the dashboard's secret settings.
  3. The list shows when each key was last used. Revoke stops a key at once.

7. Authors: build a module

A module is a short course: a pre-test, lessons made of activities, and a post-test. Open Author to see your modules and their status (draft, waiting for review or published).

  1. Start. Press New module, give it a title and a short summary.
  2. Add lessons and activities. In each lesson, choose an activity type and press Add activity:
    • Video (a YouTube link, shown without tracking cookies) and Reading (formatted text).
    • Check, Worksheet and Exit ticket: multiple choice, short answer, fill in the blank and ordering questions, each with an explanation and up to three written hints.
    • Python code (students predict, run and change real code until the checks pass) and Parsons problems (put lines of code in order).
    Tag activities with a concept (such as "loops") so instructors can see mastery per concept.
  3. Find material fast. In a lesson press Find material, type a topic, choose sources (YouTube, freeCodeCamp, Harvard CS50, GitHub) and languages, and press Add as video or Add README as reading. Videos are embedded, never copied; GitHub text is copied only when its license allows it, with the source and license written at the top.
  4. Let AI draft the tests. Under Pre-test and post-test, press Draft both tests with AI. It writes matched pairs of questions from the module's material. Check and edit every question: the drafts are a starting point, not the final word.
  5. Preview. Press Preview as student on any activity to see it exactly as a student will, with no answers showing.
  6. Submit. Press Save draft, then Submit for review. The module locks until a CIC admin publishes it or sends it back.

To change a published module, open it and press New version. This makes an editable copy; classes using the old version are not affected.

8. Instructors: run a class

Set up a class (about ten minutes)

  1. Create the class. Open Instructor → New class, name it and choose its partner school.
  2. Add students. Paste their names (first name and last initial only) and print the sheet of personal login codes. Each code is shown once, so print or save it then.
  3. Or share the class link. Show the link or QR code; students open it and choose their name. Only names nobody has claimed are listed. Make a new link after class so the old one stops working.
  4. Assign modules. Choose the published modules the class takes, in order, and when each opens.
  5. Late joiners. Use Starting point to place a student who joins mid-course at the right module.

During the course

  • Class dashboard: average progress, who is stuck (and why: wrong answers, time or days away), who has not started, mastery per concept, learning gain, and a Students list.
  • Live class view: a projector-friendly grid of every student and activity that refreshes every 20 seconds.
  • What to reteach: the questions most students got wrong first, and the wrong answers they chose.
  • One student in depth: click a name for their status, every activity, each pre-test question beside its post-test twin, mastery against the class, the mistake they keep making, an activity calendar, and suggested next steps. From there you can reset their login code, allow a test retake, download a CSV, print, or copy a drafted note to their family (you send it yourself).
  • Impact report: a one-page, printable learning-gain report for the class, ready for a funder.
A student lost their code or took someone else's name? Open the student's page and press Reset login code. The new code replaces the old one, and the student keeps all their progress.

9. Students: learn

  1. Sign in with your code, or the class link and your name. The same code works on another device; your progress moves with you.
  2. Start here shows your next step, and a catch-up plan if you missed class.
  3. Take the pre-test. It is not graded; it shows where you start. Then the lessons open, one step at a time. Your path shows every lesson and why a locked one is locked.
  4. Learn. Watch videos (with a transcript), read, answer questions and write real Python. After a wrong answer, Get a hint points the way without giving the answer. Explain it for my grade rewrites a reading in words for your grade.
  5. Take notes in the panel beside the lesson. Only you see them; My notebook collects them for revision.
  6. Take the post-test at the end. My progress shows how much you grew, and you can print a certificate for each finished module.

10. Reviewers: check the curriculum

Reviewers (for example a partner school or an external expert) open Review to read every published module exactly as students see it, with each pre-test question beside its post-test twin. Reviewers cannot see any student data or any answer key, and cannot change anything.

11. CIC's own dashboard: the read API

CIC's existing reporting tools can pull numbers directly, with a key from Admin → API keys:

AddressWhat it returns
/api/v1/completionPer class, labelled by school and term: number of students and average completion.
/api/v1/learning-gainPer class and module, overall and per concept: pre-test and post-test averages, gain and normalized gain.

Both answer in JSON, or CSV for a spreadsheet (add ?format=csv). Groups with fewer than 5 students are hidden to protect minors, and no student is ever named. Full documentation with examples is at /api/docs on the live site.

13. How polished it is

  • Tested on every change. About 800 automated tests: screens (including keyboard and screen-reader checks), the server functions, and 71 database tests that try every role against the access rules on a real database. A change cannot be merged unless all of them pass, along with type checks, a secret scan and a browser smoke test.
  • Accessible. Every screen is usable by keyboard, with labelled controls, visible focus and enough contrast. The build fails if Lighthouse scores a page below 95 for accessibility.
  • Every screen handles the awkward cases: loading, empty ("No students on the roster yet"), errors with a Try again button, and plain messages from the database such as "That login code is not valid. Ask your instructor for a new one."
  • Works on school devices: Chromebooks, phones and slow connections. Videos have transcripts for schools that block YouTube.
  • Deployed publicly on free infrastructure, with a written, tested deployment guide.

14. Safety and privacy for minors

  • Minimal data. For a student the system stores a first name and last initial, the class and the school. No email, no password, no birthday, no photo.
  • Access enforced by the database. Every request is checked by row-level security: an instructor sees only their own classes, a reviewer sees no student data, and nobody but the database ever sees an answer key.
  • AI sees as little as possible. A hint request sends only the question and the student's answer. Attention insights send only anonymous numbers. If AI fails, the student gets a written hint instead.
  • People send messages, not the system. The note to a student's family is drafted by the system and copied and sent by the instructor.
  • No secrets in the code. Keys live in the hosting settings; an automatic scan blocks any change that contains one.
  • Reporting protects small groups. The API hides any group of fewer than 5 students and never names a student.

18. Running, deploying and cost

  • Cost: $0 a month with no credit card: Supabase free plan, Cloudflare Pages free, GitHub Actions free. The first paid step, if CIC outgrows the free plans, is Supabase Pro at about $25 a month.
  • Deploy: docs/deploy.md in the repository walks through creating the Supabase project, the Cloudflare Pages site, Google sign-in, the first admin and a check that everything works, step by step. It also covers deploying by direct upload when the GitHub connection is waiting for approval, which is how the demo is deployed.
  • Settings: the browser needs only the public Supabase address and key. Server secrets (the Supabase service key, and optional AI, YouTube, GitHub and Supadata keys) live in Cloudflare's settings, never in the code. .env.example lists every one.
  • Run it on a laptop: install Node 22.12 or newer and Docker, then follow "Run it locally" in the README. With no database configured, the site runs in a demo mode with sample data.

Appendix

A. Glossary

TermMeaning
ModuleA short course: a pre-test, lessons and a post-test, about one topic.
Lesson, activityA lesson is a group of activities; an activity is one step (a video, a reading, a set of questions, a Python task).
ConceptA skill an activity or question practises, such as "loops". Mastery and reteach suggestions are per concept.
Pre-test, post-test, pairThe same skills tested at the start and end; each pre-test question has a matching post-test question (its pair).
Learning gainPost-test score minus pre-test score, in points.
Normalized gainHow much of the possible improvement a student made: a student who goes from 50% to 75% has a normalized gain of 0.5.
Seat, login codeA seat is a student's place on a class roster. A login code signs a student into their seat.
Class linkA link or QR code that lets students choose their own unclaimed seat.
VersionA published module is a locked version; changes make a new version.
Row-level securityDatabase rules that decide, for every row, who may read or change it.

B. Common questions

A staff member signed in but sees "not invited". The invite must use the exact Google email they sign in with. Invite that address from Admin → Overview.

Google says "access blocked". The sign-in screen in Google Cloud is still in testing mode. Add the person as a test user, or publish the sign-in screen.

A student is "stuck". They answered the same activity wrong several times, spent much longer than classmates, or have not been active for a few school days. Open their page for the details and a suggested next step.

Can a student use a phone at home? Yes. The same code works on any device and their progress moves with them; the last device used holds the seat.

Can we change a module while a class is using it? Make a new version. The class keeps its version until you assign the new one, so nothing changes mid-course.
